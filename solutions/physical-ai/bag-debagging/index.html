Physical AI · Bag de-bagging

Twenty-kilo sacks, lifted by a robot that sees them.

Emptying sacks of sugar by hand is heavy, dusty and endless. We’re designing a robot cell that takes the job over, working from what it sees rather than a fixed script.

Bring this jobWhere it stands
Being designed

The cell is laid out in simulation and the camera positions are chosen. No robot of ours has lifted a real sack yet.

Why it is still done by hand

What makes this job hard for a machine.

  • 01

    A sack is soft. It slumps, and no two lie the same way.

  • 02

    The stack changes shape every time a sack is taken.

  • 03

    Sugar dust hangs in the air, which is hard on people and on cameras.

How a cell does it

You give. It works out. The result.

  1. You give1 / 3

    A pallet of sacks.

    Stacked the way they arrive, not the way a robot would like.

  2. It works out2 / 3

    Where each sack is, and how it lies.

    A camera finds the top sack and its angle before the arm moves.

  3. The result3 / 3

    The sack is lifted and emptied.

    One at a time, and counted.

Before we say yes

What we will ask you.

We cannot tell whether a cell fits your floor without these. Having them ready saves a meeting.

  1. 01

    The sack: what it is made of and what it weighs.

  2. 02

    How many sacks an hour you need.

  3. 03

    The space: the layout of the cell and what stands around it.

  4. 04

    The robot you have or prefer, if any.

  5. 05

    What counts as a failure, and how often you can live with one.
